Let's break down the technical realities of comparing prices across Orca and Phoenix 🧵👇

2/8
The anatomy of spot price:
In Orca Whirlpools, price is stored on-chain as `sqrt_price_x64` in Q64.64 fixed-point format.
Formula: P = (sqrtPrice / 2^64)^2 * 10^(baseDecimals - quoteDecimals).

Spot price is only valid for an infinitesimal trade ($dx -> 0$). For actual orders, you must simulate tick array traversals.

3/8
Phoenix does away with AMM curves entirely, utilizing an on-chain, memory-mapped CLOB.
Orders match at discrete tick ladders in FIFO queues.

The result? The best execution venue changes dynamically based on trade size.

4/8
We benchmarked $1K vs $250K swaps on Solana Mainnet:
• $1,000 trade: Orca CLMM edges out Phoenix by +1.2 bps thanks to concentrated liquidity around the active tick.
• $250,000 trade: Phoenix beats Orca by +44.3 bps as deep limit-order books absorb large notional sizes better.

5/8
There is also a significant Compute Unit (CU) asymmetry:
• Orca Whirlpool: 125,000 – 280,000 CUs (walking multiple tick array accounts).
• Phoenix CLOB: ~42,000 – 50,000 CUs.

Fewer CUs mean cheaper priority fees and higher inclusion rates under heavy network load.

6/8
HTTP JSON-RPC queries are too slow for real-time price comparisons.
With 400ms slot times, public RPC reads introduce 1–3 slots of lag.

Production systems rely on Yellowstone gRPC Geyser streams for zero-copy account updates delivered in 15–30ms.
